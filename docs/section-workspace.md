# Song section workspace (v1.0.0)

Open **Load Bank D: Chopper → Chopper mode**, choose audio, check the Music Tempo BPM and grid alignment in the existing alignment editor, then choose **Open section workspace**. The ordinary marker editor and its acapella/synced paths remain available.

The workspace shares one waveform surface between **Song cuts** and **Pattern maker**. The top-left + opens Workspace options: choose one of 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16 bars, Song cuts / Pattern maker, and Source sections / Existing chops. Wildcard stays visible above the previous/next arrows. Swipe down to browse source sections; swipe up to choose a random alternative directly. In Song cuts, drag horizontally in sixteenth-note steps and tap to place a cut. Two successive cuts create an existing chop and set the proposed piece length. In Pattern maker, Source sections offers the corresponding offset in each other section. With no confirmed piece it uses the cut cursor's offset; subsequently it follows the last chosen source piece's endpoint, including any intervening silence. Existing chops offers the prepared collection, ordered by its position in the four-bar phrase.

The 16-step rhythm sits above Wildcard. A marked step begins a chop; unmarked steps lengthen the preceding chop. The loop start is always a boundary. With one bar selected, steps 1 and 9 give two half notes; 1, 5, 9, and 13 give four quarters; all steps give sixteenths. The grid stays at 16 steps as the loop grows: the individual step is a sixteenth/eighth/quarter/half/whole-bar at 1/2/4/8/16 bars. The first marker activates the rhythm and prepares the whole song with independently randomized source sections at matching musical offsets. The ↻ button rerandomizes the same template. Changing the bar length from Workspace options preserves all slots before the current playhead and rebuilds only its suffix. Rhythm can be disabled or cleared without deleting the assembled sequence. Its settings survive Done and reopening during the session.

**Rhythm mode scrubs the arrangement.** While a rhythm is active, the stage shows the whole arrangement under a fixed centre playhead instead of the candidate wheel: drag sideways to scrub, down to zoom in and up to zoom out (the point under the finger stays under it, as in the waveform view). The chop under the playhead is the one being edited, and it sounds as the playhead crosses into it (a tap or a let-go sounds it too). The ▲ / ▼ buttons (where −/+ sit without a rhythm) give that chop the next source section up (later in the song) or down (earlier) from its aligned alternatives, keeping its length and place; the arrow keys do the same (up/down), and left/right move between chops. Nothing else changes while scrubbing: no wheel to animate, no random swipe. Without a rhythm the wheel and its gestures are unchanged.

Previous/next arrows move to confirmed chop beginnings. In normal mode, choosing and confirming a candidate replaces that slot and retains the sequence suffix. In rhythm mode, vertical browsing commits the chosen source to the selected slot directly, without advancing it or changing its length. Rhythm controls the duration; note-size and −/+ buttons are disabled until rhythm is disabled. The options panel disables Existing chops while rhythm is active so the source sections remain the aligned alternatives.

A short left drag confirms the candidate. A long left drag (34% of the working width) inserts silence at the selected bottom note length instead. A short rightward drag trims exactly the selected note length from the previous piece (removing it if consumed). A full rightward drag of 34% of the working width rewinds to the beginning of the last assembled bar, crossing pieces and silence and splitting any piece at that boundary. At an exact bar endpoint this returns to the start of that completed bar. Undo and Redo restore the complete cuts/chop collection/sequence, randomized choices, rhythm configuration, and editing position. Repeat inserts an exact copy of the previous audible chop immediately before the current slot (or appends it at the sequence end), advances past the copy, and resumes aligned options; later slots remain intact. Length buttons select 1/64, 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, or one bar. Triplet sizes (marked T) appear between regular values in duration order in two fully visible rows, with larger symbol-only −/+ controls above them. Triplet buttons show red (1/3), yellow (2/3), then green (back on the matching straight-note grid); the indicators derive from timeline position, including undo and trimming. Browsing retains the chosen duration. Confirmed durations stay fixed when choosing a new length. Silence appends a gap, with a 5 ms fade-out before it and a 3 ms fade-in afterward in playback and export. The − button shortens only the candidate by a sixteenth (clamped at the shortest triplet). The + button extends only the candidate by a sixteenth, stopping at a nearer bar boundary. The playhead sits at the center with one bar of confirmed history on the left. At the append position, two thirds of the right side shows the candidate selector and the final third shows an eighth-note grid. When editing a confirmed slot, its entire confirmed suffix stays visible at the same timeline scale as the history, with the grid overlaid on it; there is no empty band while future chops exist. The grid covers at least one quarter note even for tiny candidates. A boundary within the next quarter note is highlighted, more strongly at 2, 4, 8, or 16 bars. It stays fixed during extension and loses its highlight when reached. Keyboard arrows and the Place cut / Confirm buttons provide alternatives to gestures.

Wildcard opens the full selected source section as a multicolor waveform. Tap or drag to choose a different offset, snapped to the selected note interval; the accessible position slider follows that interval too. Preview, Insert, and Cancel are explicit. Insert adds just that one chop while recording the normal aligned starting offset separately, so the next alternatives resume at the expected aligned endpoint. Cancel changes no editing state. Undo and subsequent trimming preserve that alignment metadata.

Play starts a candidate on a tap; a second tap stops it, and the next tap restarts from its beginning. Stretch is an optional phase-vocoder audition at original pitch: it extends the chop into a four-second texture and sustains it with a blended seam until stopped. It uses gentle start/stop envelopes, affects neither musical durations nor export, and keeps the playback dot at the audition start. Focus loss and hiding the app stop audio. Browsing auditions play at a constant rate, independent of finger speed. Preview Join starts four sixteenth-note steps (one quarter note) before the confirmed sequence ends, then plays the candidate without committing it. Sequence starts at the beginning and scrolls the assembled waveform and eighth-note timeline beneath a fixed center playhead, including silence. Stopping restores the editing workspace. A line shows the browsing/editing position; a dot shows AudioContext playback time. If a key offset is applied to the chopper, preview uses the same fixed pitch/speed ratio as Koala.

**Done** checks a trial Koala export before keeping the arrangement. Select the chopper pad and choose **Edit chop pattern** to reopen it during this app session. Saving a versioned native project and session restoration of the full original source are future work; export the Koala project before closing the app.

## Timing and export

- Music Tempo is the sole automatic beat detector. The single Anchor identifies a downbeat: Snap on selects the nearest existing beat, Snap off uses the exact playhead. Placing/replacing it re-detects BPM from that point; the fixed grid extends backward and forward. Manual BPM changes radiate from the anchor. Nudge grid −/+ shifts grid and anchor by 1 ms without moving audio, changing BPM, or rerunning detection. Failed detection keeps the previous BPM so manual adjustment and click playback stay available. Undo/Redo restores anchors and nudges. Manual BPM and half/double controls remain available.
- Musical positions retain the existing step unit, with fractional steps supporting regular and triplet notes down to 1/64. A 48-subdivision-per-quarter grid represents both exactly. Audio starts search for a quiet crossing within 1 ms, shared across channels, with 2 ms boundary fades. This does not move the notes off the grid or alter the original source arrays.
- An arrangement's overlapping source choices are packed into independent slices of a new 24-bit WAV. Repeated choices of the same chop and length share a slice. This prevents another chosen source boundary from ending a longer Koala slice prematurely, and avoids overwriting audio referenced by another pad.
- Arrangements use consecutive Bank D pads starting at pad 48 (D1), with up to 127 distinct pieces per pad and a separate packed WAV for each. All notes remain in one pattern, targeting the appropriate pad and local slice velocity. Up to 2032 unique pieces fit across D1–D16; exceeding Bank D capacity fails explicitly. Occupied Bank D pads prevent saving rather than being overwritten. Silence leaves a note gap. Notes retain the measured velocity-to-slice mapping, pitch 0, and 1024 ticks per sixteenth. Fractional durations round absolute start/end ticks independently, keeping a run of triplets on its bar endpoint rather than accumulating rounding errors. ONE SHOT is off so note lengths gate playback. Pattern duration is rounded up to whole bars, as required by the existing exporter; a partial final bar leaves trailing silence in Koala.
