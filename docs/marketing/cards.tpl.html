Classification

It reads folders, names, tempo and key.

Every file is typed from its whole folder path, its file name and aliases, and your saved corrections, and keeps a confidence score. Sounds nothing could type are flagged for review instead of being dropped on a random pad.

  • Eighteen Types.Kick, snare, clap, closed and open hat, cymbal, vox, perc, melodic, bass, FX, other, and the loop versions of drums, perc, melodic, bass, vocal and FX.
  • Keyed loops.A sound over five seconds with a key in its name ("Ambient_B_minor", "F# major") is a Melodic Loop, unless the name says bass or another Type.
  • Folder Types.Folders nothing could type are listed with a picker each. Your choice beats the built-in rules, and the list can be exported and shared.
Four banks

Or load each bank your own way.

Each loader fills only its bank and leaves the others alone. Sounds are named by Type and number, and the pad's Koala label uses that name. Bass, melodic loops and melodic one-shots are tuned by default.

Bank A: DrumsA pack with subfolders. Type comes from the folder name: 10 kicks, 10 snares, 5 closed and 5 open hats, 5 of every other drum type
Bank B: Melodic LoopsA folder of loops: 12 at random on the top three rows, plus 4 spares
Bank B: 808 & BassTwo basses and two 808s on the bottom row, four spares of each
Bank C: One ShotsA folder of one-shots: 16 at random, plus 4 spares
Bank D: ChopperAcapella, chopper or synced mode (below). Kit builds and the other loaders never touch bank D
Hot swap and shuffle

Every sound in your library, one tap away.

Select a pad and the screen lists the other sounds of its Type from the packs you picked, in a fresh shuffle each time it opens. Search, preview with Play, tap the swap key, or star a sound to keep it on the device for later projects (up to 1 GB). The list grows 50 rows at a time as you scroll, and only the sound you ask for is decoded.

Rows show just each sound's own name, with the pack tag and Type words taken out, and a name that repeats an earlier row's is left out. A long name scrolls while its sound plays.

  • Shuffle.Loads a random sound of the same Type from your library. Offline it uses the hot-swap pool and keeps the pad's Type.
  • Lock.A locked pad shows a padlock, and building a kit or loading a bank never touches it.
  • Fix a Type.Re-type a sound and it moves to hot swap while a sound of the intended Type takes the slot, or a labelled gap if there is none.
Pitch detection

It finds the root note for you.

Every pad is analysed on a background worker with a custom YIN tracker (de Cheveigné and Kawahara, 2002). It needs no model weights, which keeps the install small on a phone, and it is accurate on clean one-shots.

YIN's classic failure is locking onto the 3rd harmonic, which would retune a sample five to seven semitones wrong, so an early dip only counts if it is nearly as deep as the global best and frames vote on a pitch class. Melodic loops are keyed from chroma instead, as a key rather than a note. Keys are measured from the audio only, so a key written in a file name never decides the tuning.

Tune

Pick a key. Every pad follows.

  • Tap a note on the piano.Bass, melodic one-shots and melodic loops retune to it, and a tick marks every pad that moved. Drums are left alone. Tap the same note again and everything returns to its own pitch.
  • Minor or Major.Says whether the key you picked is minor (the default) or major. A loop is moved by whole semitones into the same key.
  • Tone.Plays a reference tone on the key under a pad. A sine for most sounds, a soft saw chord for a melodic loop, and the pixel knob sets its volume.
  • Written to Koala's pitch knob.Every tuning goes into the pad's pitch, to two decimals, 808s and basses included. No audio is re-rendered, so what you hear is what Koala plays.
Pitch slider

The lower your finger, the finer the control.

One slider per pad, built from scratch because iOS Safari fights native range inputs. Grab it and the pad loops at its current tuning with the reference tone on the key, and both stop when you let go.

  • Semitone mode.The value lands on whole semitones, with a detent mark for each of the 25. Any cents the pad already has are kept.
  • Tap for cents.The line bends into a tuner dial with a hand, 55 cents either side. Past that the pitch steps to the next semitone and the dial centres on it.
  • Slide down the screen.The thumb follows your finger, slowing smoothly to a tenth of the speed toward the bottom.
  • Double-tap resets.Back to the middle, in whichever mode you were in.
  • Finger above the slider.On a melodic loop, Minor and Major squares appear and the chord swaps to the relative key's.
Chop

Cut a song into a playable kit.

Bank D is the chops' bank. Three modes, all reached from the Chopper key.

  • Chopper.Any sample becomes one chopper pad with up to 127 slices, picked by velocity. A pattern maker arranges them into a new sequence, from one to sixteen bars.
  • Acapella.Give it a song and its vocal stem, from a project or as two audio files. The cuts are found on the song and made on the stem, so every section becomes a pad with its own pattern, in time and stretched to its length.
  • Synced.The same as acapella without the stem: markers go on the sample and the same sound is cut into section pads the same way.
  • Your pads, your choice.Chops go to the pads you tick on a four-bank grid, locked pads are never offered, and cuts snap to a beat. The source is checked and nothing is overwritten until you confirm.

Koala settings

Every pad set up the way Koala wants it.

Type defaults apply first, then your own Type settings, then anything you set on one pad. Loops get their length in bars, snapped to 2, 4, 8 or 16 from the name's tempo or the project BPM, because Koala does not detect it.

Colour schemes

About thirty of them, all live.

A scheme is ten tone colours for the pads and sound types, a lamp colour for the keys and switches, and an optional tint for the chassis. Pads, type keys, chops and the exported pad colours all follow it, and changing it recolours everything at once. Snare, cymbal and perc always stay well apart.
