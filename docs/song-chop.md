# Chopping a song into patterns

For acapellas and full songs: a long sample is cut into sections along a grid the user taps out, each section becomes a pad, and each pad gets a pattern that holds its note for the section's bars, so playing the patterns in order rebuilds the song.

## Flow

The chop works on the song **and its vocal stem** from Koala's stem split, the stem's pad labelled like the song's with VOCALS after it. The button (menu **Load Bank D: Chopper**, start screen **Chopper**) first asks for a mode (`AcapellaModeModal`); with no project open it then asks for a .koala file (`accept=".koala"` only: adding `.zip` made iOS grey out .koala files) and opens it as the project (the 60 s limit is ignored, no long-samples prompt).

**Acapella mode** needs the song and the stem in the open project. They are looked for with `findAcapellaPair` (`src/audio/song/stems.ts`): bank D's first two pads (song, then stem) first, then every other pad and the hot-swap pool for a pad labelled `{label}` and another labelled `{label} VOCALS`. With no pair the user is told the project needs both files. It overwrites everything on bank D (the user is warned when something is there; nothing is moved or parked), is limited to 16 chops, never touches the project's tempo once a key is picked (`SongExport.bpm` is left out, so the sections, written with stretch on and a pattern of exactly their length, follow whatever the tempo is) and leaves everything untuned. If a key was picked on the piano, `keyOffset` (`src/audio/song/keyOffset.ts`) moves the song's detected key onto it by the shortest whole-semitone distance, and that is written to Koala's pad pitch knob on every section pad (`SongExport.pitch`); the audio is never altered. Because the pads are stretched, the pitch changes without going out of time. With **no key picked** the project tempo is set to the acapella's instead (`chopSong` in `App.tsx`) and the pitch knobs stay at 0.

**Synced mode** is acapella mode without the acapella (`launchSynced` in `App.tsx`): it takes any sample over 10 seconds (the same picker as chopper mode; with no project open an audio file), the chop markers are placed on that sample and **the same sample is cut** into the section pads, so nothing is transferred to a stem. Everything after the editor is acapella mode's: bank D is overwritten (locked pads kept, warned), 16 chops at most, one pattern each, the project tempo taken only when no key is picked, the piano's key on the pitch knob. Its pads are labelled "Chop 1, Chop 2..." (`Pad.section.synced`).

**All section pads (acapella and synced)** are written by `addSongSections` with: one-shot on, **stretch on in Modern mode** (`STRETCH_MODE.modern`), `stretchLength` = the section's bars x the project's beats per bar, and a shared mute group: the song pad's own group when it has one, otherwise **5 if no pad uses it, else the first free group** (`sectionMuteGroup`; the hats of a loaded kit use 5). A section is at most 16 bars (`MAX_SECTION_BARS`) and at least one: chops always land on bar lines (`chopLines`), with Snap off too (Snap only moves the cursor), so no section is finer than a bar (tested in `chopMarks.test.ts`).

**Synced mode** is acapella mode without the acapella (`launchSynced` in `App.tsx`): it takes any sample over 10 seconds (the same picker as chopper mode; with no project open an audio file), the chop markers are placed on that sample and **the same sample is cut** into the section pads, so nothing is transferred to a stem. Everything after the editor is acapella mode's: bank D is overwritten (locked pads kept, warned), 16 chops at most, one pattern each, the project tempo taken only when no key is picked, the piano's key on the pitch knob. Its pads are labelled "Chop 1, Chop 2..." (`Pad.section.synced`).

**All section pads (acapella and synced)** are written by `addSongSections` with: one-shot on, **stretch on in Modern mode** (`STRETCH_MODE.modern`), `stretchLength` = the section's bars x the project's beats per bar, and a shared mute group: the song pad's own group when it has one, otherwise **5 if no pad uses it, else the first free group** (`sectionMuteGroup`; the hats of a loaded kit use 5). Section files are peak-normalized to `FILE_CEILING_DB` and the gain is taken off again on each pad's Koala volume knob (the loudest section sits at the vox target, the others keep their level to it, knob at most 0 dB), and each pad has a per-pad highpass (`sectionEq` in `mixPresets.ts`, 150 Hz) to stay off the bass. A section is at most 16 bars (`MAX_SECTION_BARS`) and at least one: chops always land on bar lines (`chopLines`), with Snap off too (Snap only moves the cursor), tested in `chopMarks.test.ts`.

**Pattern maker** (chopper mode only, the **Finish and open pattern maker** key under the editor's Chop key; `PatternMaker.tsx`, arithmetic in `src/audio/song/patternMaker.ts`): the chop is made as usual (one chopper pad, the project tempo set to the sample's), then a screen opens drawn like the chop editor's: a well of rows, one beat-square tall each (about 22 px, so many show at once), every row black with the chop shaded in its colour, its waveform in the screen's ink, a line in its colour where it starts, no numbers or flags and no grid lines (the grid markers, a line per bar and per beat, are drawn only on the sequence strip at the top, where the chosen slots play). The rows scroll vertically with the native snapping scroll (momentum kept) and the row in the middle of the well (the selection zone, outlined) is the one that counts. The playhead is the line a tenth of the way in from the left, with the end of the slot before shaded to its left in every row; all rows share one scale (at least 4 bars across, zooming out, eased, to fit a longer centred chop or silence). Bottom to top the rows are ordered by where in a four-bar phrase the chop began (`orderChops`): same bar as the playhead, then two bars away, then one after, then one before, each in song order; the silence row sits below the bottom chop (one bar by default, 16 at most). **A tap on the row in the zone locks it in** at the playhead (the playhead moves to its end); **a tap on any other row plays it, and a double tap scrolls it into the zone**; **a tap on the bit of the slot before that shows left of the playhead plays that slot from its beginning**; dragging along a row sets how many eighth notes of it play (one eighth to the whole chop); dragging back past nothing cuts the previous slot short (down to one eighth), puts the playhead at its end and selects it so it can be dragged out again. **Scroll** (Scroll play) plays each chop as a finger's scroll brings it into the zone. The arrows move between slots (the list scrolls to that slot's chop), locking in another chop replaces the chop in the slot, **Remove** drops it. A transport plays the sequence (`renderSequence`): Play from the playhead, Last bar, Start, Stop and a volume knob. **Done** keeps the sequence on the chopper pad; the export writes it as ONE pattern (`ChopperExport.pattern`: a note per chop slot at `timeOffset` 2048 ticks per eighth, velocity of the chop's slice, silence leaves a gap). When a slot is cut short or silence is used the chopper's ONE SHOT is written 0 so the note length ends the chop (assumed from the one-shot behaviour measured with `probe-chopper.koala`; not yet checked in a render). The project tempo is the sample's, as for any chopper, so the pattern matches on export.

**Chopper mode** works on any sample over 10 seconds in the project (`ChopperSourceModal`, the hot-swap pool included) and writes ONE pad using Koala's own chopper (`src/audio/exportChopper.ts`; the format is read from `docs/calibration/chopper-reference.koala`): `type "synth"`, `synth "CHOPPER"`, the slice start frames in `pad.chops.slices[]`, and `ONE SHOT` 1, `MONO` 1, `PLAY THRU` 0 and `TRIGGER MODE` 1 (velocity picks the slice; 0 is the key played) in `synthParams`. It shares the song's sample (the WAV is only written again if the export dropped it). Slice 0 always starts at frame 0, and a slice starts where the last section ends. Up to 127 slices; the velocity of a note picks the slice (`sliceVelocity`), and each section gets a pattern with one note at its slice's velocity (the first 32 sections; the rest still play from the pad). The chopper is not stretched, so it plays at the sample's own tempo: the project tempo is overwritten with the sample's, and a key offset (pitch knob) multiplies it by 2^(semitones/12). Changing the pitch or Koala's tempo afterwards takes it out of time. It takes the first free pad of bank D and replaces an earlier chop. Confirmed from a project Koala saved after opening `docs/calibration/probe-chopper.koala`: `TRIGGER MODE` 1 is velocity, and a synth pad's colour lives in `synthParams.padParams.color` (a top-level `color` is dropped). The render of the probe also measured: velocity v plays slice floor(v x slices / 128) (`sliceOfVelocity`), the note's own pitch transposes the slice (so it is written as 0), the pad pitch knob is in semitones and changes speed and pitch together (+2 shortened a 0.9 s slice to 0.80 s), and ONE SHOT 1 plays the whole slice however short the note while ONE SHOT 0 cuts it at the note's length.

The sections go on **bank D only**, and a new chop replaces the last one. The song is what the grid is tapped on (it is the full mix, with the beat to tap to); the vocal stem is what is cut.

1. A sample over 60 seconds gets the "Long samples" prompt on import. **Chop to patterns for acapella** sits under each sample, next to Delete (tapping it on the song or on the stem does the same).
2. **The check** (`checkStems`, `src/audio/song/stems.ts`) runs first. The stem must be a pad whose **label** in Koala is the song's label with ` VOCALS` after it (label, a space, VOCALS in capitals; case and any extension are ignored; a pad with no label is called by its sample's file name). It must also be the same length as the song to within `STEM_LENGTH_TOLERANCE_S` (30 ms): a stem whose start or end point was touched comes out a different length. If anything is wrong an alert says what to do: do the stem split in Koala and don't touch anything on the stem (its start and end points, and so on), because it has to line up with the song to the sample. Nothing opens until the check passes.
3. `SongChopModal` is one screen (v0.9.0). Nothing is cut until **Chop** is pressed, because the cuts are rendered into files and cannot be corrected afterwards.
4. Chop runs in a fixed order, each step only once the one before it is done (`chopSong` in `App.tsx`, v0.17.6):
   1. **Cut**: the vocal stem is cut at the cuts (`scalePlans` puts the sections on the stem's own frames if its sample rate differs) into section pads (`makeSectionPads`, `src/audio/songPads.ts`) on bank D's free pads (an empty slot or a blank **Empty pad** placeholder). Bank D is kept for the sections alone: no tool ever puts a sound or a placeholder there (see CLAUDE.md). Sections that find no free pad are dropped (16 at most).
   2. **Trial write** (`trialWriteSections`): the sections are written into a fresh copy of the Koala project exactly as the export writes them (WAV, pad with stretch, pattern, tempo) and read back; every section must have its pad, its audio and a pattern.
   3. **Fill the pads** with the sections, and if "tune the project to the song's key" is ticked set the project key to the tonic (the key is found on the song in the background, `analyzeSong`, as a suggestion; the tempo and bar 1 come from the user).
   4. **Delete** the vocal stem's pad and the full song's pad (the export drops their samples).
   5. **Last, the drum layout** is switched on with the MPC layout (the first of `FINGER_LAYOUTS`; a layout already on is kept), so bank A is the empty kit waiting for a drum pack (Load Bank A, or a dropped folder). The layout keeps the sections where they are (`arrangeInto` carries them over, as does turning the layout off, `removeLayout`).

   If step 1 or 2 fails, an alert says why and **nothing is changed**: the song, its vocals and the editor's markers are all still there.
   The sections are **left alone**: they are virtual pads (`Pad.section`, not `isReal`), so Organize, tuning, normalizing and Mix never touch them, and they go on no bus. Each is labelled **Vox 1, Vox 2...** (`sectionLabel` in `App.tsx`) and coloured with the palette colour it had in the chop editor, kept as hex on the pad (`section.color`, so a later palette change does not recolour it). The label and the colour are written to Koala whatever the Organize switch says. The bank loaders work after a chop and never touch bank D.
5. Export (`addSongSections`, `src/audio/exportSong.ts`) writes a 24-bit WAV and a pad per section (one-shot, one shared choke group, **stretch on, `stretchLength` the pattern's bars in beats**: see "Stretch" in `docs/koala-mixer-reference.md`), the patterns (`bars` long, the note held for all of them) and the project tempo (the grid's). Patterns go in the free pattern slots; `autoPlay: "next"` chains them.

## The editor

The panel is styled like the rest of the app (Graphite · Barlow): a chassis panel (`--chassis1`) with keycap buttons (`.chop__btn`, the same gradient and edge as the pad keys; a pressed or on toggle sinks and lights its legend in the accent), an orange primary **Chop** key, the sections in a well, and the waveform with the time, tempo and last action on a small OLED screen (`.chop__screen`, Silkscreen) as the only pixel-font part. The knob is lit with the accent.

The tempo and bar 1 come from the automatic detection (`analyzeSong`, run in the worker when the editor opens; `baseGrid` in `src/audio/song/chopMarks.ts`). Bar 1 is the first bar line whose downbeat is a big peak (`firstBigBar`, `beats.ts`: at least half the song's median downbeat strength), so the soft hits of an intro do not count as where the music starts. Which beat of the bar is the 1 is picked by where the bass lands hardest (`pickDownbeat`); when that is not clear (a syncopated 3-3-2 kick ties two beats) the first big bass hit of the song (`firstBigBeat`, which may be a kick right at the file's start) is taken as the 1. If no beat is found a plain 120 BPM grid is used and the downbeat markers do the work.

The waveform (`ChopTimeline`, `src/components/ChopTimeline.tsx`) scrolls behind a **cursor line fixed in the middle**. Dragging is Ableton style (`zoom.ts`): sideways drags the waveform and the point under the finger stays under it; **zoom only starts when the finger leaves the waveform vertically**: held below its bottom edge zooms in, above its top edge zooms out (the same ratio for every equal step, from the edge; back inside the waveform the zoom returns to where it started), so there are no zoom buttons. The line stays in the middle and follows the finger freely while dragging. **Snap** acts only on release: the line glides onto the nearest bar line (`bounds` and `settle` in `ChopTimeline`: every bar when a bar is at least 30 px wide on the screen, otherwise every fourth bar counted from the grid's first bar; never finer than a bar, and markers do not pull). While the song plays there is no snap. The grid and the waveform are drawn from the same view, so they move together. Only **bar lines** are drawn, never anything finer, and the lines drawn are exactly the lines the snap goes to (`gridLines` in `ChopTimeline`): every bar when a bar is at least 30 px wide on the screen, otherwise every fourth bar, and at the widest views every sixteenth (the longest a section may be), counted from the grid's first bar. A line disappears when it stops being a place the snap can go.

- **Play / Pause** plays from the cursor with a **click on every beat, always on** (higher on the first beat of a bar), the waveform scrolling under the cursor. **Pause leaves the line where the song was and glides it onto the nearest bar line.** Dragging the waveform while the song plays pauses it for the drag and **carries on playing from where the line is on release (no snap)**; dragging while paused snaps on release as usual. The **Click** knob (`Knob.tsx`: drag up or right to turn it up) sets the click volume (the gain follows the square). Beside it, **◀ 1 / 1 ▶** jump the line one bar back or forward along the bar lines and **◀ 4 / 4 ▶** four bars (from a bar line the whole distance; from between two, the bar line already ahead counts as the first); if the song is playing it carries on from there.
- **Chop marker** puts a cut at the cursor, snapped to the nearest **bar line** (`barLineNear`). The sections are the bars between neighbouring cuts (`sectionsBetween`), listed with their length in bars and given the palette colours in order. Audio before the first cut and after the last is not kept. A section over 16 bars (`MAX_SECTION_BARS`) is flagged and Chop stays disabled until a cut is added inside it. Tapping a section in the list moves the cursor to its start.
- **Auto markers**: a section may not pass 16 bars. While the song plays a chop marker is added where the section since the last chop reaches 16 bars, and a chop added far past the last one fills in a marker every 16 bars. **Chop by 8** and **Chop by 16** replace the chops with one every 8 or 16 bars from the first chop (or first bar line) to the end of the song, then chop at once.
- **Downbeat marker** says a bar starts at the cursor (moved onto the sharpest attack within 20 ms). **1.1.1** says which bar is bar 1 and also puts the first chop marker there (below). Every downbeat marker is an **anchor** (`gridWithMarks`, `chopMarks.ts`); the 1.1.1 is one only while there is no downbeat marker. Once there is one, the 1.1.1 never moves the grid or the tempo: it is put exactly on the nearest bar line, not on a transient, so a quiet intro cannot skew the BPM. A marker sits on a transient only if the rise stands `ATTACK_CONTRAST` above the window's level, otherwise exactly at the cursor. The earliest anchor is where bars are counted from, and the lines **tile backwards from it as well as forwards**, so a 1.1.1 set midway through the song gives the intro its grid too (the first downbeat of the song is found by scrolling back). With one anchor the detected tempo is kept. With more, the **tempo is fitted through all of them** (a straight line of anchor frame against bars counted, whole bars between neighbours worked out with the tempo so far), so the BPM homes in on the exact one as markers are added; the grid is one tempo for the whole song, each anchor only re-locking the phase from there. The fit may not stray more than 15 % from the detected tempo. A marker less than half a bar from the one before is the same bar and is left out. With no anchor, the detection's own bar 1 and tempo stand.
- **1.1.1** also puts a **chop marker** on it, and it is the **first chop marker**: chops before it do not count and cannot be added (the first section starts on it); moving or removing the 1.1.1 moves or removes that chop with it, and a chop you place there by hand stays.
- **÷2 and ×2** next to the BPM take the detected tempo half or double (kept with the markers, so undo covers it). **Snap on/off** turns the magnet off, to put a downbeat marker exactly where the sound is when the grid has gone out of line.
- Pressing a marker button where one already is **removes it** (a chop on the same bar line; a downbeat or the 1.1.1 within a quarter of a beat). **Undo** and **Redo** step through everything placed (`History` in `chopMarks.ts`).
- Markers are kept as the frames they were placed on and the grid and the cuts are worked out from them, so a downbeat marker or 1.1.1 added later re-snaps every chop to the corrected bar lines.

The tap-tempo editor (tapping the beat, microphone knocks, the drift check, nudging single lines) was taken out at v0.9.1; it is in the history at v0.8.3.

## Exactness

`planSections` rounds every section's two ends from the lines' exact positions, so sections picked end to end share a frame and nothing is lost or doubled (tested frame by frame, also with a nudged line, a fractional beat and a tempo change). A section's `bars` is its length in whole bars, at least one, for its pattern; one that is not a whole number of bars is still cut where the lines are, held for the nearest whole bars, and the summary says which (`oddSections`). Audio outside the sections is not kept. The project tempo the export writes is the grid's at the first section's first line (`bpmAt`); a section that crosses a tempo change is still cut where the lines are.

## A locked layout

The editor panel has a fixed size (`.chop` in `App.css`, inside the safe areas: `.chop-backdrop` pads the top by `--safe-top` and the bottom by `--safe-bottom`, so the header clears the status bar), every line of text above the controls sits in a slot of fixed size (the note is always two lines, the readout two single lines cut off rather than wrapped), and scroll chaining and text selection are off inside it, so nothing moves or resizes while a finger is on the waveform. Check this when adding to the editor.

## Limits

The chop is not remembered across a page reload (the song pad comes back from the project file). Chop and export in one go. Between downbeat markers the grid is rigid: a live drummer who drifts needs a marker where it goes out. The marker arithmetic is tested; the editor's touch handling has not been tried on a phone yet.

The older editor (detected tempo and bar 1, draggable markers that refined the grid, snap on/off) is in the history at v0.5.6.


**Playback.** In Koala every section pad is a one-shot, all in one choke group (a section cuts the one before it), routed to bus D (Melodic). In Tune God a section previews like a melodic loop: it loops while the pad is held and stops on release (`padMode` in `App.tsx`).
