# Chopping a song into 8-bar patterns

For acapellas and full songs: a long sample is cut into 8-bar sections, each section becomes a pad, and each pad gets a pattern that holds its note for the 8 bars, so playing the patterns in order rebuilds the song.

## Flow

The project always has the song **and its vocal stem** from Koala's stem split, the stem's pad labelled like the song's with VOCALS after it. The song is what the cuts are found on (its transients give the tempo and make the points easy to set); the vocal stem is what is cut.

1. A sample over 60 seconds gets the "Long samples" prompt on import. **Chop to patterns for acapella** sits under each sample, next to Delete (tapping it on the song or on the stem does the same).
2. **The check** (`checkStems`, `src/audio/song/stems.ts`) runs first. The stem must be a pad whose **label** in Koala is the song's label with ` VOCALS` after it (label, a space, VOCALS in capitals; case and any extension are ignored; a pad with no label is called by its sample's file name). It must also be the same length as the song to within `STEM_LENGTH_TOLERANCE_S` (30 ms): a stem whose start or end point was touched comes out a different length. If anything is wrong an alert says what to do: do the stem split in Koala and don't touch anything on the stem (its start and end points, and so on), because it has to line up with the song to the sample. Nothing opens until the check passes. The label is read from the pad (`KoalaPadRef.label`, `Pad.label`), not the sample's file name, and the long-sample list shows it.
3. `SongChopModal` finds tempo, bar 1 and key on the song (in the analysis worker, `analyzeSong` in `src/audio/song/beats.ts`) and shows them as suggestions. Nothing is cut until **Chop** is pressed, because the cuts are rendered into files and cannot be corrected afterwards.
4. Chop cuts the **vocal stem** at the song's cuts (`scaleGrid` puts the grid on the stem's own frames if its sample rate differs) and replaces the stem's pad with section pads (`makeSectionPads`, `src/audio/songPads.ts`) on free pads: the fourth bank first, then the others from the back. The song's own pad stays. Sections that find no free pad are dropped. If "tune the project to the song's key" is ticked the project key is set to the tonic.
   The sections are **classified as vocals** (category `vox`): they are labelled with the song's title (its label in Koala), the app's vocal label and the number, `Toxic Vox 1`, `Toxic Vox 2`... (`sectionLabel` in `App.tsx`; the small caption on the pad is just `Vox 1`); with Organize on they take the vocal colour, and with Mix on they go to the vocal bus, like any other vocal sound. With those off, the stem pad's own colour and bus are kept.
5. Export (`addSongSections`, `src/audio/exportSong.ts`) writes a 24-bit WAV and a pad per section (one-shot, one shared choke group, **stretch on, `stretchLength` the pattern's bars in beats** (Koala counts the stretch length in beats: 8 bars of 4/4 is 32) so the vocals stay in time if the tempo changes, cloned from the stem's own pad), the patterns, and the project tempo. Read from a real project: see "Stretch" in `docs/koala-mixer-reference.md`. Patterns go in the free pattern slots (an empty one has `notes: null`); `autoPlay: "next"` chains them. The project's `beatsPerBar` is kept (or the editor's, when changed).

## The grid

`SongGrid` (`src/audio/song/chop.ts`) is bar 1, a base tempo, the bars in each section (8 unless the song's structure says otherwise) and **anchors**: the cuts the user has placed by hand. Every cut placed refines the grid, working from left to right:

- A cut with an anchor is where it was put. The cuts **between** two anchors are spread evenly between them. The cuts **after the last** anchor follow the tempo fitted through all of them (a least-squares line, `framesPerBarOf`), starting from the last one. With only bar 1 the tempo is the base tempo; once any cut is placed the tempo comes from the cuts (`effectiveBpm`, the project tempo the export writes) and the tempo box locks to it.
- More cuts make a better fit, so one cut put a little off counts for less.
- **Sections of unusual length do not disturb the grid.** A section that is 5 bars, or 4, only changes how many bars lie between two cuts (`SongGrid.bars`); the tempo is fitted from where cuts are against how many bars they are apart, so a 5-bar section placed on the beat leaves the tempo exactly as it was. A cut that is **not** nearly a whole number of bars from where the grid had it (more than 0.08 of a bar off) is read as a tempo change instead, and refines the tempo. Only cuts placed by hand count: the grid never learns from the cuts it works out itself. Lay a few cuts at the start, then one at the end: every cut between is spread evenly and the ones after follow the fitted tempo, so they come out within a frame or so for a steady song (tested).
- **The snap toggle** (`Snap on/off`, off by default). **Off:** a marker is placed exactly where it is dragged, live, and every one placed refines the grid after it. **On: the grid is locked.** A dragged marker jumps from bar line to bar line (`snapCut`), drawn on the nearest one while it is dragged (the waveform moves in steps with it); nothing is placed until it is let go. Letting go (`snapPlace`) moves only that marker: the grid, its tempo and every bar line stay exactly as they were, and so does every other marker. The section before it gains or loses the bars it crossed and the section after it loses or gains the same, so each other marker keeps its bar line (with no marker after it only the section before changes), and the marker never crosses its neighbours. If it had been placed by hand and so held the grid in place, what it taught the grid is kept as a place the grid passes through that no marker is tied to (`SongGrid.fixed`). With snapping on, the controls that would change the grid are disabled: the nudge buttons, Snap to grid, To transient, Reset cut and Reset grid, the tempo and beats-per-bar boxes. Bar 1 does not snap, as it is where the grid starts. Turning snapping off again, a marker moved freely refines the grid as before. The grid lines on the waveform come from the grid alone (`gridLines`), so a marker moving never moves them. A marker that was the playhead snaps where playing stopped.
- **Snap to grid** takes the chosen cut's anchor away: it goes back to where the other cuts say it belongs. **To transient** moves it onto the nearest sharp rise in loudness (within 30 ms), which makes it an anchor. **Reset cut** also undoes a structure change the cut caused; **Reset grid** clears every anchor and structure change.

## Exactness

`planSections` rounds every cut from its exact position and each section's end from its exact start, so with no cuts placed by hand the sections tile the song with no gap and no overlap (tested frame by frame). A section lasts exactly its bars at the song's tempo; a cut placed by hand can leave a gap or an overlap of the few milliseconds the song drifts. The last section is padded with silence to a full 8 bars. A downbeat before the start of the file pads the first section with silence. Audio before bar 1 is not kept.

## Detection

- Tempo: autocorrelation of the spectral-flux onsets (bass band weighted in, so hats between the beats do not double the tempo), then a straight-line fit through the onset nearest every predicted beat. Accurate to a fraction of a BPM on a steady song, not on one that drifts.
- Bar 1: beat phase is reliable; which beat is beat 1 is a guess (bass weight), then snapped onto the sharpest attack within 30 ms. The editor's note says when to check it.
- Key: chroma from prominent spectral peaks (tuning-corrected) against Krumhansl profiles. A bare triad can read as its relative minor or major.
- All of this is tested on synthetic songs only. Check real music against the grid before trusting it.

## Editor

`SongChopModal` is drawn in the OLED's colours (black, the screen ink, Silkscreen headings) but not on its pixel grid, so the waveform is full quality. `ChopTimeline` shows about half the song at rest (`defaultSpan`), with every cut as a tab along the bottom whose top comes to a point. Constants to tune are at the top of `src/audio/song/zoom.ts` (`MIN_SPAN_FRAMES`, `MIN_ZOOM_ROOM_PX`) and `ChopTimeline.tsx` (tab size, `RETURN_MS`).

- Dragging a tab works the way Ableton's markers do. The marker **keeps the place on the screen where it was grabbed** for the whole drag. Sideways, the **waveform follows the finger** (`moveMarker`): the point of the waveform under the finger stays under it, so dragging right pulls the waveform right and the cut ends up earlier. A pixel covers `span / width` frames, so the further in, the finer, and at the closest zoom a pixel is about a frame.
- **Zoom is vertical and gentle.** Drag **down** to zoom in and **up** to zoom out (`spanAfterDrag`, a constant ratio per pixel, from whatever zoom the view had when it was grabbed). Nothing zooms until the finger has passed a vertical **dead zone** (`ZOOM_DEAD_ZONE_PX`, 24 px, `zoomTravel`), so a sideways scrub that wanders up or down never zooms; past it zoom starts from nothing with no jump. Going from the resting view to the closest takes the whole way from the finger to the bottom of the screen (`zoomRoom`). Zooming scales the view about the marker and never moves it.
- **Let go and nothing snaps back**: the marker is placed and the view, zoom included, stays as it is. Zoom out by dragging a marker up.
- **Dragging the waveform itself** (no marker grabbed) scrubs and zooms the same way and changes no marker: the point of the waveform under the finger stays under it (`viewUnderFinger`), so it follows the finger sideways, zooms about that point as the finger goes down (in) or up (out), and has the same tap threshold and vertical dead zone as a marker.
- Cut 1 is bar 1 and starts the grid. Any other cut, once moved, is an anchor (see The grid): the grid after it follows from where it was put.
- **A bar missing (or added).** A song does not always keep to 8 bars: a pre-chorus can drop a bar. When a cut is let go almost exactly one to seven bars (within 0.08 of a bar) from where the grid has it, `settleCut` takes that as the song's structure: the section before it is that many bars shorter (or longer, up to 16), the cuts after it follow (`SongGrid.bars`), and the cut stays an anchor where it was put. That section's audio is exactly that many bars, and its pattern is that many bars long with the note held for all of them (`SongExport` section `bars`). A cut that is merely a bit off the grid (a drifting tempo) just refines the tempo. **Reset cut** also undoes the structure change a cut caused; dragging a cut back to where the grid had it restores the 8 bars.
- Nudge buttons (-10 ms, -1 and +1 frame, +10 ms), Snap to grid and To transient act on the chosen cut.
- **Taps and centring.** Tapping a tab (the finger goes no further than `TAP_SLOP_PX`, 8 px) selects it, moves nothing and brings it to the middle of the view; a drag only starts once the finger passes that, and carries on from where it is, with no jump. The arrows under the waveform choose the previous or next chop point and centre it (they stop at the first and last). The view may run past either end of the song by half its width (`clampViewStart`), so bar 1, often a second in, can be centred and zoomed in on.
- **Playing** (the big button under the waveform, owned by `ChopTimeline`): **hold** to play; it fades out when let go, the way a pad does (`PadHandle.release`). **Slide down** before letting go to lock it on (`LATCH_DRAG_PX`); press again to stop. The beat **clicks** (toggle beside the button) follow the refined grid, scheduled a quarter second ahead of the playhead.
- **Where playing starts.** Once the waveform has been touched (dragged, scrubbed, zoomed) there is a **playhead line in the middle** of the view and playing starts from it, whatever is under it. Once a chop point is touched, tapped, moved, chosen with the arrows or nudged, **it is the playhead** instead, and playing starts from it. While playing from either, the view follows with the playhead in the middle, and when it stops the view goes back to where it was.
- **A marker held while playing is the playhead.** Press play with a second finger while one holds a marker: playing starts at the marker, its line stays in the middle of the view whatever the finger does (the finger can still zoom by moving down), and the waveform scrolls under it. When playing stops the marker keeps the place it got to (committed with `onMoveCut`), slides from the middle back to the place on the screen where it was grabbed, carrying the waveform with it (`settleUnderFinger`, an exponential approach, `SETTLE_TAU_MS`), and then the drag carries on from there. Pressing play again, finger still down, starts from the marker's new place. If the finger is lifted while playing, the cut is placed (and settled for structure changes) when playing stops.
- The waveform comes from a min/max pyramid (`src/audio/song/waveform.ts`), so a 7-minute song draws in full at every zoom without scanning the audio.

## A locked layout

The editor panel has a fixed size (`.chop` in `App.css`), every line of text sits in a slot of fixed size (the note above the waveform is always two lines, the cut readout two single lines that are cut off rather than wrapped), the labels that change do not change length, and scroll chaining and text selection are off inside it, so nothing moves or resizes while a finger drags the waveform. Check this when adding to the editor: a line that can wrap differently while dragging moves everything under it.

## Limits

The chop is not remembered across a page reload (the song pad comes back from the project file). Chop and export in one go.
